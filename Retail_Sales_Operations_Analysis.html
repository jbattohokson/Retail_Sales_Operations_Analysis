Executive Summary

This is a case study dataset from a bike and accessories retailer selling in the US, France and Germany. Monthly revenue averaged about $45K from December 2014 through July 2015, then jumped to about $440K in August 2015 and averaged roughly $470K a month through June 2016. The jump held, so it reflects a real change in the business rather than a one-off spike.

The growth hid a margin problem. US Bikes ran a -1.7% gross margin, so the retailer lost about $10K at the gross profit line on roughly $570K of US bike revenue. Germany sold the same categories at 16.7% on bikes.

The fix starts with US bike pricing. Accessories, already the largest US category at $2.35M, carry up to 22% margin on Fenders and Bike Racks and are the natural add-on at bike checkout.

Analysis

What the data shows

01August 2015 was a lasting shift, not a seasonal spike

Revenue stayed under $55K a month from December 2014 through June 2015, rose to about $145K in July, then reached about $440K in August. It stayed between roughly $355K and $650K every month after that.

July 2016 drops to about $210K. The data ends on the week of July 31, so that month is likely incomplete and I excluded it from trend modeling.

Interpretation

Something changed in mid-2015: a new channel, a pricing change or a market expansion. The data confirms the shift but can't name the cause. Finance should identify the business event and use the post-August level as the forecasting baseline. A Chow test would formally confirm the break.

02US bikes lose money at the gross profit line

US Bikes brought in roughly $570K at a -1.7% gross margin, about $10K in gross losses before operating costs. Bikes are a small share of US unit volume (about 1,800 bikes vs. 20,000 accessories), but they carry the highest ticket price, and on average they sold below cost.

What this means for pricing

A US bike pricing review is the highest-value action in this dataset. The question for the pricing team: what increase on US bike SKUs reaches breakeven at current volume, and where does lost volume start to offset it? Germany's 16.7% bike margin on the same categories points to a US pricing or cost problem, not something built into the category.

03Accessories carry the margin, and the best ones are under-sold

Accessories are the largest US category by revenue ($2.35M) and units. Fenders (21.5%) and Bike Racks (21.9%) have the highest margins of any sub-category but small revenue: about $280K and $26K across all markets.

What this means for merchandising

Offering Fenders and Bike Racks at bike checkout raises blended margin without a price change. The direct upside is modest: a 10% attach rate on ~1,800 US bikes at $50 each is about $9,000 in revenue and roughly $1,300 in gross profit at the US accessories margin. Pricing is the bigger lever. The attach offer is a low-cost test to run alongside it.

04Germany beats the US on margin by 15+ points in every category

Germany's margins run 30.2% on Accessories and 28.5% on Clothing, about double the US in both, and 16.7% on Bikes vs. -1.7%. That's an 18.4-point gap on bikes alone.

What to find out next

The gap comes from price, cost or product mix, and each points to a different fix. Higher German prices on the same SKUs mean US underpricing. Lower German costs (freight, distribution, duties) mean an operations opportunity. A richer accessories mix means a merchandising opportunity. Pulling unit price and unit cost by SKU and market would separate the three.

0525 to 34 year olds drive volume, and gender splits are close to even

The 25 to 34 band is the largest at about $1.94M, roughly 20% above 35 to 44 (about $1.61M). Revenue falls off sharply after 55.

What this means for targeting

Women make up a slightly larger share of revenue from age 45 up, but the splits are close enough that this is a hypothesis to test, not a targeting rule. An A/B test of the checkout accessories offer by segment would show whether any group responds differently.

Statistical Models

Two OLS models and their limits

M1Time series: does revenue trend up over time?

A linear regression of monthly revenue on a time index.

R² = 0.674Slope = +$30,760 / monthp < 0.001n = 20 months

Read before using this number

The residuals are negative early, positive after August 2015 and sharply negative at the end. That pattern means the model is forcing one straight line through two different periods. The slope works as a whole-period average but shouldn't be used to forecast.

M2Demographic model: what drives revenue per transaction?

A cross-sectional regression of revenue on age band, gender, product category and quantity.

Limitation

The categorical variables are label-encoded, which treats categories as ordered numbers. The coefficients can't be compared as effect sizes until the model is refit with one-hot encoding. Until then, it doesn't support segment-level targeting.
